The challenge

A town worth a weekend, marketed like an afternoon.

Bisbee is a still-living copper mining town a mile up in Arizona’s Mule Mountains: independent shops, galleries, live music and a historic district with no chains. It is also about 90 minutes from Tucson, which makes it an easy day trip, and day-trippers don’t fill hotel rooms or keep weekday businesses open.

Residents told us what they wanted marketing to do: sell Bisbee as an overnight, multi-night destination; fill the summer slump and the Tuesday-to-Thursday lull; target Phoenix and Tucson first; and grow without turning into a theme park. They were equally clear about what they didn’t want — generic stock, AI imagery, or the word “quirky” in a headline.

Phase 1 was discovery: understand who comes and why, find out what the official website does for them today, and set a creative direction the whole campaign could build on.

Phase 1 goals
  • Overnight and multi-night stays
  • Mid-week and summer visitation
  • The right visitors, not the most visitors
  • A website that finishes the trip

Design research

Let the town speak first.

Before any visuals, I turned the raw inputs into artifacts the client could read in a minute. The survey’s open-ended comments became a weighted term map; the competing persona sets became a single crosswalk that shows where every source agrees.

Insight · a word to retire

“Quirky” came up eight times, but one respondent warned that “a lot of us are put off by the word.” Fine for describing the town, risky in a headline. It never appears in our copy.

Insight · the real ask

Locals want overnight stays, not more traffic: fill the summer slump and weekdays so businesses can stay open, and keep Bisbee from becoming “Bisbeeland.”

Insight · creative watch-outs

Use real Bisbee art, with each artist’s permission, and real photography. No billboards built around one person’s face.

One cast, four sources

The strategy deck, AOT’s high-value segments, the community survey and the focus group each described Bisbee’s visitors differently. The crosswalk reconciles them: personas set the message (why they come), focus-group profiles set the media (who, and from where). Every campaign is briefed as Persona × Profile.

The hinge · north star

Bisbee is a real, authentic, welcoming, living town, not a theme park. The closer you look and the longer you stay, the more it gives back.

Real over polished

The people are the attraction: lifers, makers and musicians, never posed models or generic stock.

Deeper over faster

Past Main Street, into Warren, the canyons and the late show. Every message earns one more stop or one more night.

Weird, with respect

Elevated in every sense, and always grounded in real places and residents. Quality over quantity.

The test for every piece of work Does it make someone want to look closer, or stay longer?

The hidden metaphor: Bisbee was built by people who dug for what others couldn’t see. The same instinct, curiosity rewarded, now powers the visit.

Website audit

Auditing the front door.

Social sells the feeling; the website closes the trip. We crawled every sitemap URL, ran an automated WCAG 2.1 scan, tested speed on real and throttled connections, searched the site the way visitors would, and planned trips on it as each persona. Every finding is shown on a screenshot of the live site.

Creative direction

Elevated, in every sense.

I built a moodboard for each focus-group visitor profile — who they are, their itinerary, how the trip should feel, imagery cues and what we still need to shoot — plus a shared visual language that makes every persona’s imagery unmistakably Bisbee: hard high-desert light, a deep cobalt sky, a hand-drawn layer over photography and lettering with personality.

Two looks, one chosen

Phase 1 presented two distinct directions, each with type, palette and tone of voice. Artsy carried forward into the final deck; Destination Focused was archived as Look B.

Artsy Selected

Honors Bisbee’s history with an accent on personality. Vaguely nostalgic but not played out — “There’s more to Bisbee than you think,” with a wink. Warm and low-key, like tungsten bulbs in an old saloon.

How it was made

An AI-augmented workflow, with judgment kept human.

Built in Claude

The decks, moodboard layouts and synthesis artifacts were designed in Claude, moving from raw survey data and site crawls to client-ready slides in one connected workflow.

Sourced, not invented

Every figure carries its source on the slide, and assumptions are labeled as assumptions to validate with GA4 and Phase 2 research.

Real over generated

In line with the community’s ask, no AI imagery: references guide the direction, and production will use original Bisbee photography and local art.
